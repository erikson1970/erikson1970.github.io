    Things to try

    Reactor follows the turbine. Run the Throttle 100 → 40% drill at 5×. Tcold rises as the steam generator takes less heat, reactivity goes negative, and power drops to match. Tavg ends where the rods put it; only the Thot–Tcold spread and steam pressure change.

    Slow pumps cap power. Start from Quiet running, then open the throttle past about 50%. Half the flow means twice the ΔT for the same power, and the flow-biased trip stops you near 63%.

    Check valves matter. Tick RCP-2 check valve stuck open, then stop RCP-2. RCP-1 pushes part of its flow backwards through the idle pump, and core flow falls well below what one pump should give.

    The pressurizer is a spring. Move the rod program 302 → 296 °C. The loop shrinks, water drains from the pressurizer, pressure sags, and the heaters come on to recover it. Try Spray valve stuck open to see how fast spray can pull pressure down.

    Everything needs electricity. Set ship's service to 3 MW and trip TG-1: load shedding saves the bus. Try again at 4 MW: shedding isn't enough, the breakers open, the pumps coast down, the reactor scrams, and the plant goes over to natural circulation.

    The sea sets the efficiency. Compare Arctic and Tropical on the efficiency chart, then try Lose seawater pump and watch the condenser lose vacuum.

    How the plant model works

    Not a real plant. All values are generic, textbook-scale PWR figures (150 MW thermal, about 155 bar, Tavg about 302 °C) chosen for teaching. No naval reactor design data is used or implied.

    Reactor: point kinetics with one delayed-neutron group (prompt-jump approximation, β = 0.0065, λ = 0.1 s⁻¹), a moderator temperature coefficient of −50 pcm/°C (typical late in core life), a 4 s fuel time constant and three decay-heat groups (6.5% of power at shutdown). The rod program sets the core average temperature the reactor settles at; rods drive toward a new setpoint at 0.05 °C/s (3 °C/min) and never run the program more than 4 °C ahead of the actual core temperature, which caps how much reactivity a cooldown can add. After a scram reset the program restarts from the loop's current temperature, so the plant heats back up at that rate instead of jumping. With no Doppler term, steady Tavg equals the setpoint at any power, so steam demand alone sets power and the Thot–Tcold spread.

    Primary loop: 58 well-mixed cells around the loop (core, upper plenum, hot leg, steam-generator U-tubes, cold leg, downcomer), 20 m³ of water. Pump heat goes into the cold leg. The two pumps run in parallel: each has a head curve, the loop has a square-law resistance, and the flow split is solved every step. A check valve blocks reverse flow through an idle pump; a stuck-open one lets the running pump push water backwards through it. With both pumps stopped, natural circulation from the hot/cold density difference keeps a few percent flow.

    Pressurizer: a 6 m³ vessel of saturated water and steam in equilibrium, solved from its mass and internal energy with steam-table data. When the loop heats up, water expands into it (insurge) and compresses the steam bubble; when it cools, water drains out (outsurge) and pressure falls. Heaters boil water to raise pressure. Spray brings cold-leg water into the steam space, condensing steam and dropping pressure; spray flow needs the pumps' head to drive it. There is no charging or letdown, so loop inventory is fixed.

    Steam generator and turbines: the secondary side is a boiling pool at saturation with perfect level control and 120 °C feedwater. The throttle sets main-turbine steam flow and moves at 1%/s, the turbine loading rate; flow is capped by what the wide-open valve can pass at the current steam pressure. Cycle efficiency is 62% of the Carnot limit between steam and condenser temperatures, so colder seawater gives a colder condenser and more shaft power per megawatt of reactor heat. The condenser is a shell-and-tube unit (UA 5 MW/K) cooled by 3.5 t/s of seawater at fast speed. Above 60 °C (about 0.2 bar back-pressure) the main turbine trips; above 70 °C the turbine generators trip too.

    Electric plant: two 3 MW turbine generators share the bus and draw steam in proportion to load. Loads are the coolant pumps (0.5 MW each at fast, power ∝ speed³), the seawater pump (0.4 MW), pressurizer heaters (up to 0.8 MW) and ship's service. Overloading the online generators for more than 3 s trips their breakers. A dead bus stops every pump and heater.

    Protection: a relief valve on the steam generator lifts at 92 bar and vents up to 20 kg/s to atmosphere, the only heat sink once the turbines and condenser are lost. On a generator overload, non-vital ship's service load is shed to 40% after 1 s; if the bus is still overloaded at 3 s the breakers open. After a scram reset, rods withdraw automatically but never add more than 0.4 β of reactivity, which keeps the startup period at about 15 s or longer.

    Trips: the reactor scrams on loss of power to the coolant pumps, high power (118%), power above the flow-biased limit (110% × flow + 8%), pressure above 170 or below 125 bar, subcooling margin under 5 °C, pressurizer level above 92%, or a short period. A scram also trips the main turbine.
