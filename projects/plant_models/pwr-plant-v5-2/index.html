Operating limit: rod withdrawal stops above 100% × flow + 3. Fast rod insertion above 100% × flow + 8. Scram above 110% × flow + 8, after a 10 s grace period with the rods running in.     New in v5.2

    Limit board. Every protective function in one table: reactor trips, fast rod insertion, rod withdrawal stops, and the steam and electric automatic actions. Each row shows the live value, the setpoint, the action and its state, and can be bypassed. A bypassed function is logged and shows as an alarm.

    Smarter arming. The low-pressure trip is blocked during post-scram recovery, until the plant is back above 145 bar and 10% power, so pulling rods on a depressurized plant no longer causes a second scram. Low steam generator level is blocked below 2% power, where only decay heat is being removed. The startup-rate rod stop is 2 DPM below 1% power and 1 DPM above.

    Fast rod insertion. Some upsets now drive the rods in fast (2%/s for up to 4 s) instead of scramming: power above the limit for the current pump line-up, a startup rate above 3 DPM, and a subcooling margin under 10 °C. The flow-biased scram waits 10 s after a fast insertion starts, so a pump loss at power can be ridden through by bringing the throttle down. The maximum-power table on the board gives the limits for each pump line-up.

    Plant changes. Pressurizer 9 m³ (was 6) with 1.2 MW of heaters and more spray, normal level 60%. Charging tank 6 m³ and charging and discharge at 3 L/s (were 3 m³ and 1.5 L/s). Manual rod drive has regular (0.15%/s) and fast (0.6%/s) speeds in both directions.

    Things to try

    Reactor follows the turbine. At 10×, drop the propulsion throttle from 100% to 40%. Tcold rises, moderator feedback pulls power down to match, and the rods in Auto trim Tavg back to the program. Watch the reactivity chart: moderator, Doppler and rods trade off while the total stays near zero.

    Xenon. Run at 1000×. After a power drop, xenon first rises (less burnout) and the rods withdraw to hold Tavg; it takes hours to settle. Start from Restart 4 h after scram: xenon is about 1.7 times its equilibrium value, and the core goes critical only with the rods near 87% out. Around 8 hours after a full-power scram, xenon peaks and there isn't enough rod worth to restart at all.

    Scram and recovery. Press SCRAM. Nothing trips the propulsion turbine, so close the throttle yourself and close the shore breaker to take the electric load off the generators; otherwise steam demand keeps cooling the plant. Reset the scram, then drive rods Out in Manual. The rod stop holds the startup rate at 1 decade per minute while the log meter climbs. At a few percent power, open the throttle to about 15% and select Auto.

    Inventory. Drop the rod program from 302 to 290 °C. The loop shrinks, the pressurizer drains, and Auto charging pumps water in from the charging tank through the sample loop. Raise it again and the discharge valve sends water back.

    Steam generator level. Use the Loss of feed drill. The level falls toward the top of the U-tubes at one third of the shell; below that the tubes uncover, heat transfer drops, and the reactor trips at 30%.

    One generator. Use Trip TG-1. TG-2 picks up both sides through the tie bus; the frequency dip sheds non-vital ship's service until it can carry the load.

    Both generators. Trip TG-1, then shut TG-2's steam valve. The non-vital buses die, the panel opens both vital/non-vital breakers, the coolant and seawater pumps restart in slow on their vital windings, and the MGs turn around to run the vital buses from the battery. The reactor trips on low flow; closing the throttle is your job. Watch the battery minutes. To recover, reset the TG trips, wait for Power Good, close the TG breakers, then the vital/non-vital breakers.

    DC voltage. Put DC control in Manual and drop the MG voltage setting below the battery's open-circuit voltage: the battery discharges and the MGs push power toward AC, unloading the generators. Raise it and the MGs charge the battery from the AC side.

    How the model works

    Not a real plant. All values are generic, textbook-scale PWR figures (150 MW thermal, about 155 bar, Tavg 302 °C) chosen for teaching. All values are derived from published, publicly available sources.

    Neutronics (Level 1 + decay heat): point kinetics with six delayed-neutron groups (U-235, β = 0.0065), integrated with the prompt-jump approximation and a small neutron source so a shut-down core sits at about 10⁻⁸ of full power. Reactivity is the sum of rod worth (a sigmoid integral worth curve, 8% total), Doppler (−2.5 pcm/°C on a lumped fuel temperature), moderator temperature (−30 pcm/°C at 302 °C on the core coolant, fading linearly to zero at 100 °C as it does in a real core) and xenon-135, all measured from the full-power equilibrium state with rods at 70%. Iodine-135 and xenon-135 follow the standard production, decay and burnout equations; equilibrium xenon is worth about 2.6% and peaks roughly 8 hours after a shutdown. The fuel is one thermal node (4 s time constant) feeding the core coolant cells. Decay heat uses four groups totalling 6.5% of power at shutdown.

    Rods: in Auto, rods move up to 0.3%/s to hold Tavg on the program (which itself moves at 3 °C/min), with a power-mismatch term (reactor heat against heat taken by the steam generator) and startup-rate damping, as in real PWR rod control. Auto is meant for the power range: with no steam load it inserts rods, so startups are done in Manual. In Manual, In/Out drives at 0.15%/s. A rod stop blocks withdrawal above 1 DPM startup rate or 103% power. A scram drops the rods in about 2 s and puts rod control in Manual.

    Primary loop and pressurizer: 58 well-mixed cells around the loop. Water density varies with temperature, so heating the loop pushes water into the pressurizer (insurge) and cooling pulls it out (outsurge). The pressurizer is solved as saturated water and steam from its mass and energy with steam-table data. Two pumps run in parallel with check valves; natural circulation remains with both stopped.

    Sampling loop and charging: the reactor coolant pumps drive a side stream from pump discharge through a recuperator, a seawater cooler and a 0.5 m³ sample tank, and back through the recuperator to pump suction. The sample valve draws from mid-height. The 3 m³ charging tank is at atmospheric pressure and fully mixed. A fixed-displacement pump (1.5 L/s) charges into the top of the sample tank; a discharge valve lets the same rate back out. In Auto, charging holds pressurizer level within ±2% of the setpoint.

    Steam generator: a boiling pool with its own water inventory and energy balance. The U-tubes fill the bottom third of the shell, and heat transfer falls in proportion to how much of the bundle is uncovered. Feed control in Auto matches steam flow and corrects level to 50%. A relief valve lifts at 92 bar.

    Turbines: propulsion steam passes the main steam valve, the propulsion shutoff valve and the throttle, all operator-controlled; nothing trips the propulsion turbine automatically. Cycle efficiency is 62% of the Carnot limit between steam and condenser temperatures, so colder seawater means more shaft power. Each turbine generator has rotor inertia, a governor with a 60 s spin-up ramp and a 3.3 MW steam limit; overload slows it, and under-frequency (below 97% speed for 2 s) or loss of steam with shore power connected (reverse power) opens its isolation breaker. Low condenser vacuum (above 70 °C) trips the generators.

    Electric plant: two AC sides, port (odd numbers) and starboard, each with a non-vital and a vital bus joined by a vital/non-vital breaker. The non-vital buses meet at a tie bus through port and starboard tie breakers, and shore power feeds the tie bus through its own breaker; it can run in parallel with the generators, which then drop to 0.3 MW each. Each turbine generator feeds its own non-vital bus through an isolation breaker, so one generator can carry both sides through the tie bus. Breakers are switched on the one-line diagram. Coolant and seawater pumps have fast windings on the non-vital buses and slow windings on the vital buses, each through an automatic bus transfer (ABT); the feed and charging pumps are on the vital buses through ABTs. If a fast winding loses power, the pump restarts in slow. Each vital bus drives a motor-generator. Its AC machine is locked to the AC frequency, so its DC field excitation decides which way power flows: a setting above the DC bus voltage pushes power into the DC bus, below it draws power from the DC bus toward AC. With no generator on its AC side, an MG inverts and runs its vital bus from the DC bus. MGs carry 1.0 MW continuously and up to 1.5 MW; above 1.0 MW an overload timer trips the AC breaker after 3 minutes (faster above 1.5 MW). The DC breakers only open by hand. The battery is 5000 Ah at 250 V (open-circuit 232–266 V, 2 mΩ); in Auto the MG setting keeps it on a trickle charge or discharge in the 4000–4500 Ah band.

    Trips: the reactor scrams on high power (118%), power above the flow-biased limit (110% × flow + 8%), startup rate above 4 DPM, RCS pressure above 170 bar or below 125 bar (the low-pressure trip is armed only above 5% power, so a shut-down plant can be reset), subcooling margin under 5 °C, pressurizer level above 92%, steam generator level below 30%, or loss of power to both coolant pumps.
