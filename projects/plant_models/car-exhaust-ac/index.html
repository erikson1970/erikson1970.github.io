Engine, exhaust and generator

Exhaust heat follows engine load: plenty on the highway, little at idle, none with stop-start. The generator's thermal mass smooths it out, but takes minutes to warm up.

How the model works

The trip. A drive cycle sets the speed every second. Road load is aerodynamic drag, rolling resistance, grade and acceleration. The engine's efficiency falls at light load and idle. About a third of the fuel energy leaves as exhaust at 260 °C (idle) to about 700 °C (full load). An exhaust heat exchanger after the catalyst passes part of it to the generator, which has its own thermal mass. A bypass valve protects the generator from overheating.

The machine. Each working pair uses the same characteristic-temperature model as the Einstein Refrigerator Lab, sized to the rated cooling at 35 °C air and a 5 °C coil. Cooling is limited by three things: the lift margin (generator against absorber and condenser, which sit at the grille), the exhaust heat times the COP, and a start-up lag. The start-up lag is long for diffusion machines, whose hydrogen has to circulate. Machines that rely on gravity (bubble pump, gravity drains) lose capacity when grade, braking, acceleration or cornering tilt their liquid levels by more than about 3°. Air-cooled lithium bromide crystallizes when the absorber passes about 40 °C.

The cabin. The cabin has two thermal nodes. The air node takes conduction through the body and glass, ventilation and leakage, occupants (85 W each), and a fifth of the solar gain. The interior node (seats, dash, trim) absorbs the rest of the sun and trades heat with the air. A hot soak in the sun before the trip sets their starting temperatures. With fresh air, the coil must also condense moisture, which takes cooling away from the cabin. The thermostat throttles cooling near the setpoint.

The comparison. The reference car has a normal 6 kW compressor air conditioner (COP about half of Carnot), driven off the engine at its marginal efficiency, and it cannot use stop-start with the AC on. The heat-driven car pays only for its pump and fan, the extra mass it carries, and a little exhaust back-pressure.

Not modelled. Engine warm-up and catalyst light-off, battery-electric cars (no exhaust), reheat and humidity control, and detailed refrigerant charge dynamics.
