How the model works

Flow path: the primary fluid passes through the inner tubes of HX-1, HX-2 and HX-3 in turn, and enters the top of the tank. HX-1 and HX-2 are the cooler (cooling water in the annulus) and the recuperator (tank return in the annulus). The order buttons choose which one comes first. HX-3's annulus carries an independent service stream in counterflow. It cools the primary when the service fluid is colder and reheats it when the service fluid is hotter. Fluid leaves from the tank bottom, goes back through the HX-2 annulus in counterflow, and exits the system. The mid-height valve takes a sample out of the system, so the flow back through HX-2 equals the primary flow minus the sample flow.

Exchangers: each tube and annulus is split into 60 well-mixed cells, the same transient model as v1.0 (2 L in the tube and 4 L in the annulus per unit). Each unit has its own UA. Cooler first / Recuperator first changes the order the primary meets them. Swap units exchanges which hardware (A or B) does which job, and each unit keeps the fluid it was holding. Unit C always sits in HX-3.

Why the order matters: a recuperator only moves heat in proportion to the temperature difference between its two streams. Placed after the cooler, its feed is already almost as cold as the tank return, so it does very little. Placed first, it sees the hot inlet against the cold tank return, does most of the work for free, and leaves the cooler a smaller job. The tank ends up colder and the cooling water warms less, but the system outlet leaves hotter: the recuperator moves heat from the feed into the discharge instead of removing it.

High temperatures: properties are constant and every stream is treated as liquid. Water above 100 °C or glycol above about 106 °C triggers a pressure warning, because those loops would need to be pressurized. Use thermal oil for a realistic unpressurized 300 °C loop.

Tank: 24 horizontal layers with plug flow downward. Heat is lost to ambient through each layer's share of the wall, plus the lid and base, with U = 1 / (R·0.1761 + 0.17) W/m²K. The 0.17 m²K/W term covers the inside and outside surface films, so even R-0 has finite losses. In Stratified mode, layers exchange heat by conduction, and any layer warmer than the one above mixes upward (buoyancy). Stirred mode keeps the tank uniform.

Skip to steady state: solves both exchangers' own steady-state equations directly, then steps only the tank, with large time steps, until nothing changes. It lands exactly where the live model would settle. This matters because a large, well-insulated tank can take days to settle.
