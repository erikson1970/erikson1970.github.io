How the model works

Loads. Heat loss is the envelope coefficient (Btu/h·°F per sq ft) times the area. Monthly heating is that coefficient × HDD × 24 × 0.85, where the 0.85 accounts for internal and solar gains beyond what the 65 °F base already assumes. Cooling is coefficient × CDD × 24 × 1.6, which adds solar gain and latent (dehumidification) load. Design loads use the design temperatures. Zones split the area for the per-zone figures and add zone-control cost.

Hot water. Use is 12 + 17 gallons a day per occupant. Cold-water mains temperature follows the U.S. DOE Building America correlation from the monthly air temperatures, which lags and damps the air swing.

Ground. The undisturbed temperature at depth y is the surface mean plus the geothermal gradient, plus a seasonal wave that shrinks as e−y/d and lags by y/d radians, where the damping depth is d = √(365·α/π). The surface mean and swing come from the climate table, plus 2 °F for snow and ground cover. Below about 30 ft the season disappears.

Loop. Each month the net heat pulled from (or pushed into) the ground is spread over the total bore length. The ground answers as an infinite line source around each well (exponential-integral solution), plus the interference from its neighbours, which are laid out in a row at the given spacing. The model superposes every month's load step over 20 years, so the field slowly cools when it gives up more heat in winter than it gets back in summer. Entering water temperature is the undisturbed bore temperature minus that ground response and a 0.15 h·ft·°F/Btu borehole resistance. The design-hour check adds a six-hour peak pulse at the design heat load.

Heat pump. Heating COP and capacity rise with entering water temperature (+0.03 COP and +0.8% capacity per °F, from the rating at 32 °F). Cooling EER falls by 0.25 per °F above 77 °F. When capacity is below the design load, the shortfall comes from resistance backup, estimated from how far short it falls. The loop pump draws 80 W per ton while the compressor runs.

Current system. The oil or propane boiler burns fuel at its seasonal efficiency for space heat. It heats water through an indirect tank at 85% of that efficiency. Burner and circulators use 5 kWh per MBtu. Fuel energy content is 138,500 Btu/gal for oil and 91,500 for propane. CO₂ is 22.4 lb/gal for oil and 12.7 for propane. Window units meet about 70% of the cooling load at their CEER and are replaced every 5 years. The boiler is replaced once, in the year you set.

Air-source refit. A cold-climate ducted heat pump is sized so its output at the heating design temperature covers the design load. Its COP and capacity vary linearly between the 5 °F and 47 °F ratings. Each month is split into nine temperature bins (±2 standard deviations of 8 °F around the monthly mean). Any shortfall below the balance point comes from resistance heat. A monthly model understates the coldest nights, so real backup use will be somewhat higher. Hot water comes from a heat-pump water heater at COP 3.3. Outdoor units last the number of years you set (15 is typical in New England) and cost 45% of the original equipment to replace.

Costs. Prices escalate at the rates given. The 20-year card adds maintenance (escalating with electricity) and shows the present value at the discount rate. The heat pump unit typically lasts 20–25 years and the bore field 50+, so no replacement is included.
